# Bloom

**Live app:** [bloom-cal.vercel.app](https://bloom-cal.vercel.app) · Sign in with Google and it just works.

## Summary

Bloom is a cute, cloud-synced life planner I designed and built solo — calendar, tasks,
habits, notes, journal, goals, and a focus timer in one warm workspace. It's live right now,
and a public launch on **web, iOS, and Android** is in the works (which is why the codebase
is private for the moment).

## The idea

Students and workers juggle classes, tasks, habits, and notes across a pile of cold,
disconnected apps. Bloom pulls daily life into one friendly place — and makes it feel
*pleasant*: soft colors, three themes (Light / Dark / Sakura), custom accent colors, and
little celebrations when you actually do the thing.

## The part I'm proudest of: sync that disappears

Bloom is **offline-first**. Every change saves to local state instantly (Zustand +
localStorage), then debounce-syncs to Supabase in the background. Realtime subscriptions
push changes back out to every signed-in device — so you can add a task on your phone on
the bus, and it's already on your PC when you sit down. Theme, notes, habits, everything
follows your account.

- **Auth:** Google sign-in via Supabase Auth
- **Data:** PostgreSQL with 10 tables, each guarded by Row Level Security — user data is
  isolated at the database level, not just in app code
- **Offline:** PWA with Workbox — the app keeps working with no connection and reconciles
  when you're back
- **Safety:** Markdown notes are sanitized with DOMPurify

## Design decisions I care about

- **Forgiveness-based habit scoring.** Instead of a streak that resets to zero the day life
  happens, habits carry a 0–100% consistency score that bends without breaking. Kinder, and
  more honest.
- **Mood–habit correlation.** Log your mood daily and Bloom's statistics show how it moves
  with your habits over 30 days.
- **Speed as a feature.** A global `Ctrl/Cmd+K` quick-add creates a task, event, or note from
  any page in about two seconds.
- **A focus timer that follows you.** Start a Pomodoro session and a mini widget keeps
  ticking in the corner as you move around the app — with lo-fi, rain, or fireplace
  background sounds.
