# Le Pathétique (BearHacks 2026)

**DevPost:** [devpost.com/software/le-pathetique](https://devpost.com/software/le-pathetique)
**GitHub:** [github.com/Cengizbey-m/Le-Pathetique](https://github.com/Cengizbey-m/Le-Pathetique)

A pretentious French food critic reviews your cooking out loud, then hands you three
recipes to redeem yourself. Built solo at BearHacks 2026 over one weekend.

## The pitch

Upload a photo of whatever sad meal you are looking at. The app works out what is actually
in it, then a French-accented voice delivers a critique somewhere between disappointment
and fury. Then it offers a way out: three recipes tagged broke, decent, and fancy, so you
can fix dinner at whatever budget you have.

## The pipeline

1. Photo upload in the browser.
2. **Google Cloud Vision** labels what is in the picture, so the model does not confidently
   review a bouillabaisse when you are holding a granola bar.
3. **Gemini Flash** takes the labels and the image and writes both the critique and the
   three recipes in a single multimodal pass.
4. **ElevenLabs** synthesizes the critique in a French accent.
5. **Web Audio API** layers in sound effects generated in the browser, with no audio assets
   to download.

## The decision that saved the demo

Hackathon Wi-Fi is a coin flip and API keys have a habit of failing at 2 AM. I built an
offline mock mode: if Vision or Gemini fails, the app falls back to canned but plausible
critiques and recipes and the demo continues. Judges never see a spinner that does not
resolve.

That was the right call for the same reason it would be in production. A user-facing
feature that depends on three third-party services needs a defined behaviour for when one
of them is down.

## Other things I am happy with

**Six personas, 13 easter eggs.** Repeat visits surface different critics with different
voices and hidden interactions, which gives the demo replay value instead of one laugh.

**A deliberately ugly interface.** Most hackathon projects share the same look: Tailwind
defaults, soft shadows, gradient buttons. I went the other way with serif headlines, hard
borders, and monochrome with a single ink-red accent. It stood out on a table of forty
similar-looking laptops.

**One call instead of two.** Gemini handles the visual analysis and the creative writing
together, rather than chaining a separate vision model into an LLM. Fewer moving parts,
less latency, fewer failure modes.
