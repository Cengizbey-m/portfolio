# TheTripMan (Client)

**Live site:** [trvoo.com](https://www.trvoo.com)

## Summary

A booking and payments platform I built solo for a transportation business with a
1.2M-follower audience. It is live, it takes real customer payments, and I still maintain
it. I work on it under a revenue-share agreement, so I earn a commission on every booking
the platform processes.

## The problem

The business had reach but no way to convert it. Bookings arrived as Instagram DMs and
phone calls, payment happened in person or not at all, and nothing was recorded anywhere
searchable. Every reservation cost the owner a conversation, and no-shows cost real money
because nothing was collected up front.

They needed customers to book and pay without a human in the loop, and they needed one
place to see what was actually scheduled.

## What I built

**A self-serve booking flow.** Browse services, pick a time, enter details, pay, receive a
confirmation. No message thread required.

**Stripe, wired properly.** Payment is the part that has to be correct. A reservation is
only confirmed once Stripe's webhook says the payment settled, not when the browser thinks
it succeeded. That distinction is what stops the database from holding bookings that were
never actually paid for.

**An admin dashboard.** Authenticated, and built so the owner can manage every reservation
without contacting me. The goal was to make myself unnecessary for daily operations, which
is what a client actually wants.

**Automated confirmations.** Transactional email through Resend, with calendar invites for
both sides. This removed the manual follow-up that previously happened for every booking.

**Two scheduling modes.** An embedded scheduler and a custom calendar, because the business
runs differently in peak season than off season.

## Tech

- Next.js 15 (App Router), TypeScript
- Tailwind + shadcn/ui
- Stripe for checkout and webhooks
- PostgreSQL (Neon) with Prisma
- Cal.com / FullCalendar / Google Calendar sync
- Resend for transactional email, GA4 for analytics
- Vercel, with Jest and jest-axe accessibility tests behind Husky hooks

## Why it matters

This is not coursework. It is a production system with a paying client, real money moving
through it, and consequences when something breaks. I own it from the schema to the deploy
button, which means I have had to think about things coursework never asks: what happens
when a webhook arrives twice, how to change a live schema without downtime, and how to ship
a fix on a Saturday because bookings do not pause for weekends.
