# Puffy (Client)

**Live site:** [puuffy.ca](https://www.puuffy.ca)
**Repo:** [github.com/Cengizbey-m/Puffy](https://github.com/Cengizbey-m/Puffy)

## Summary

The website for a boutique patisserie on Spadina in Toronto, and my second paying client.
I built it before the shop opened and I still maintain it now that it has. The point of
having it here is range: I can work with a large influencer-driven business like
TheTripMan and also take a neighbourhood shop from nothing to a clean, fast online
presence, then stay with it.

## The problem

The shop had not opened yet. That sounds easier than it is, because a business with no
storefront has no foot traffic to fall back on: until the doors open, the site is the only
thing anyone can find. It had to do the job a storefront window normally does, which is
tell you what they make, where they will be, and when you can turn up.

Since then the doors have opened, and the site's job has changed. It is no longer a
countdown to a launch, it is the thing people check before they decide to walk in.

## What I built

A fast, mobile-first site that answers the three questions customers actually have: what do
you sell, where are you, and when are you open.

Mobile-first was not a style choice. Almost nobody researches a dessert shop on a desktop.
The layout was designed at phone width first and expanded from there, and the build was
kept light so it loads quickly on a phone connection.

The structure is simple enough that the owner can grow into it: menu, location, hours,
contact. The address and hours are deliberately easy to swap in, because the lease and
fit-out were still being finished while the site went up.

## Since opening

The shop is on Google Search and Maps now, so it turns up for people searching locally
rather than only for people already walking down Spadina.

## In progress

- A QR-code menu so customers can scan in-store and browse on their own phone.
- Menu and content updates as the kitchen's offering settles after opening.

## Tech

Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, deployed on Vercel.

## Why it matters

Not every job needs a 1.2M-follower client. Puffy is the project that shows I can scope
work to what a small business actually needs, ship it cleanly, and keep it practical
instead of over-engineering a five-page site.
